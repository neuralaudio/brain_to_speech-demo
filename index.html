Mid-Depth Speech Representation Targets Maximize Invasive Brain-to-Speech Decoding

Dept. of Electrical & Electronic Engineering, Yonsei University, Seoul, South Korea

Abstract. Many brain-to-speech (BTS) synthesis systems utilize intermediate speech representations, but little prior work has studied how to best leverage these features within a consistent and unified setting. We address this gap by conducting a comprehensive comparison of semantic and acoustic representations for sEEG-to-speech decoding. Controlling for frame rate, vocabulary size, quantization, and decoder architecture, we show that semantically informed speech representations provide superior decoding performance than reconstruction-based codec tokens, spectral representations, and source-filter features. We also perform layer sweeps across five self-supervised learning (SSL) model backbones, finding that optimal decoding performance comes from the use of mid-depth representations rather than those from extremely deep or shallow layers. Furthermore, neither parameter scaling nor target-language pretraining provides significant gains, and pooling representations from adjacent layers does not reliably outperform a well-chosen single layer. While the results of end-to-end waveform synthesis remain largely unintelligible, we find that recognizable words can be discerned from participants with high Cohen's κ, with decodability correlated with speech-responsive contact counts.

Decoding setup shown here

Stage 1: sEEG → unitsBand-averaged high-gamma envelopes with Wiener channel gating → 4-block Conformer (d=144) → HuBERT-Base layer-9 k-means units at 50 Hz. Ten training seeds per participant; the ensemble averages their log-posteriors.
Unit priorTrigram over the units, estimated from the cohort's training speech runs (add-0.5), applied as a hybrid HMM: emission log p(s|x), transition β·log p(st|st−2,st−1) with β=0.5, forward-backward frame MAP within each speech run.
Stage 2: units → waveformPredicted units are replaced by their codebook centroids, mapped from participant-standardised to LibriSpeech feature statistics, and rendered by a HiFi-GAN generator trained on LibriSpeech to map continuous layer-9 features straight to 16 kHz audio. Every column uses this same generator.
ColumnsOriginal: recorded speech in the evaluated window. Continuous Original: the true layer-9 features through the generator (synthesis ceiling). Quantized: true features quantised to the cohort codebook (quantisation cost). sEEG Decoded: units predicted from brain signal by the ensemble with the prior.

Δκ is the chance-corrected speech-frame token accuracy over the no-sEEG template. Tiers use the k=10 Δκ of the ensemble-plus-prior decoder: High ≥ 0.30, Medium 0.10–0.30, Low < 0.10. The three samples per participant are the test trials with the highest k=10 decoded frame accuracy; the frame accuracy shown per row is that of the decoded units at that k. Expect most clips to be unintelligible: the paper reports intelligible words only for the participants with speech-responsive electrode coverage.

Data. The recorded speech, the intracranial EEG it was decoded from, and the word prompts come from Verwoert, M., Ottenhoff, M. C., Goulis, S. et al., “Dataset of Speech Production in intracranial Electroencephalography”, Scientific Data 9, 434 (2022), doi:10.1038/s41597-022-01542-9, distributed on OSF (osf.io/nrgx6) under the Creative Commons Attribution 4.0 licence. Changes from the source: the Original clips are excerpts of the dataset’s audio, which its authors pitch-shifted by a fixed 1–3 semitone offset per participant to preserve anonymity, resampled to 16 kHz and trimmed to the evaluated window; all other clips are synthesised. Participant labels P01–P10 correspond to sub-01–sub-10.

All clips are 16 kHz mono WAV under audio/, referenced by relative path; the page has no external dependencies. Original, Continuous Original and Quantized clips are the paper's evaluation renders; sEEG Decoded clips were rendered for this page from the ensemble-plus-prior unit streams through the same generator. Built from scripts/build_audio_demo.py; manifest in manifest.json.
